# Tutii Brand Kit

This is the working art-direction and production brief for Tutii across Gurudevi's educational continuum. It extends the existing character bible and mascot asset pipeline. It does not replace the lotus identity, the shared mascot renderer, or the established Gurudevi tokens.

## The idea

Tutii is a small, attentive firefly who helps people orient themselves and stay with the next useful step. She belongs beside people, not above them. She is a companion to learners, educators, school leaders, families, and communities; her presence does not imply that she knows a person's private thoughts or replaces a trusted human relationship.

Her emotional range is broad, but her energy stays composed. She can be curious, focused, delighted, uncertain, quietly concerned, playful, or at rest. She does not talk constantly or demand attention.

## Brand relationship

- **Gurudevi / lotus:** the enduring place, identity, and possibility of growth.
- **Tutii / firefly:** the responsive guide who helps people find their way through the place.
- **People:** the reason the system exists and the center of every community scene.

Tutii is not the logo, app icon, or a substitute for human characters. A joint wordmark lockup is optional for editorial or campaign work; app identity remains governed by the existing Gurudevi mark.

## Art direction

### Core anatomy to preserve

- One recognizable firefly silhouette and one face across all roles and situations.
- Plum-violet head, warm ink contour, natural translucent wings, delicate antennae, and a small botanical green detail around the thorax.
- A contained abdomen light that reads as a firefly signal, not a golden aura.
- Expressive eyes and limbs are allowed; head-to-body ratio and eye size must stay calm enough for adults and children to identify with her.
- Her gaze, wing angle, antennae, lean, and hand gesture carry the emotion before props do.

### Grounded rendering

Use the existing Gurudevi palette and scene tokens as the source of truth: vellum and warm ink, living greens, muted plum, and restrained saffron/apricot. Do not invent a separate gold system. Natural school gardens, shaded verandas, trees, books, desks, water, stone, and daylight can place her in a lived world. Avoid fantasy temples, glowing portals, star fields, giant halos, glitter, and decorative lotus thrones.

Aim for tactile editorial illustration with a slightly imperfect, organic line and controlled dimensionality. Keep the warm surface, but vary the colors and materials in the environment. Avoid baby-like proportions, sticker outlines, plastic 3D gloss, canned emoji expressions, and visual references that copy another company's mascot or collection.

### Role representation

Tutii does not change species or put on a job costume. Her orientation, gesture, light level, and the surrounding scene can acknowledge who is present. Use human characters when a situation is specifically about a learner, teacher, principal, parent or guardian, or community. Show a mix of ages, genders, abilities, family structures, and culturally ordinary settings without turning identity into a prop.

### Personal appearance variants

Tutii may have two user-selectable body silhouettes: **Soft** (rounder, fuller abdomen) and **Swift** (slimmer, more tapered abdomen). These are two expressions of the same companion, not separate characters. Keep the head, face, antennae, wings, botanical thorax detail, palette, name, voice, role meaning, and motion grammar consistent. Neither form is the default for a particular age, role, gender, or ability; both receive the same product moments and availability.

The variants should be implemented as a small number of alternate body layers in the approved master. Expressions, gestures, contexts, and motion should be authored once and shared. This keeps the library from doubling for every choice. A user preference can select the form, with a stable neutral default and an easy way to change it.

| Audience / context | Tutii's useful behavior | Scene cues |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learner | invites curiosity; waits while they think; marks a next step | study, practice, reading, classroom participation, reflection |
| Teacher | offers calm orientation; shares attention with the class | preparation, lesson planning, attendance, feedback, classroom care |
| Principal / school leader | quietly connects people and information | school overview, staff coordination, planning, community conversation |
| Parent / guardian | welcomes and clarifies a school connection | family space, notices, conferences, learner support, events |
| Librarian / mentor | follows a question or points toward a resource | library, discovery, reading, guidance |
| Community / school team | makes room for shared work and celebration | events, arts, sport, service, meetings, welcoming a new family |

### Asset production priority

1. **Identity foundation:** approved front and three-quarter masters, side silhouette, face/expression map, wing and light construction, proportion sheet.
2. **Core reusable poses:** welcome, listen, curious, guide, rest, interrupted, complete, flying/arrival.
3. **Context scenes:** learner, teacher/classroom, school leadership, family, library, community/event, wellbeing, and recovery.
4. **Scale and theme exports:** inline, card, scene; light and dark. Simplify detail at small scales rather than scaling scene artwork down.
5. **Motion clips:** arrival, idle/listen, attention shift, guide, fly-by, settle, and quiet completion.
6. **Campaign extensions:** school-year seasons, open days, community celebrations, and editorial stories, all built from the locked master.

## Motion design

Motion expresses intention, not decoration. It should answer what Tutii is doing in that moment.

| Motion family | Character action | Suitable use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrive | enter from a short, plausible arc; angle toward the person; slow down and settle | first introduction and selected narrative moments |
| Attend | blink naturally, make one small gaze or antenna adjustment, then wait | listening and quiet presence |
| Guide | lean or drift toward a relevant destination, gesture, then return to a stable pose | orientation and recovery |
| Fly | wing cycle, body pitch, and short arc share one coordinated rhythm | rare travel between narrative beats; avoid as a continuous idle loop |
| Rest | wings relax, body light remains visible, breathing is minimal or absent | reading, waiting, offline, and low-attention surfaces |
| Recognize | one restrained brighten-and-settle gesture | meaningful completion or shared contribution |

For the requested first introduction: Tutii enters first, rotates or leans into a direct gaze, settles, then the introduction and name question appear in order. She remains present and attentive while the person responds. The user's touch, progress, or screen state can drive her behavior; the app does not need camera-based tracking.

### Format choices for 2026

| Format | Keep it for | Strength | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SVG | identity marks, diagrams, small scalable vectors | crisp at every size and inspectable in source control | not the right container for every textured scene |
| PNG / WebP | textured illustration, scene art, still fallbacks | broad compatibility and good control of image fidelity | a flat image cannot genuinely blink or change gaze |
| Lottie JSON / dotLottie | authored timeline moments with a defined beginning and end | compact playback for arrival, transition, and celebration | keep state decisions in the app; avoid making Lottie a second product state machine |
| Rive | a genuinely interactive, layered character rig | can blend character states and respond to named inputs | add only after the rig is authored and the native runtime passes a compatibility proof |
| Reanimated | UI-thread timing for small movement around a rendered asset | coordinates arrival, drift, settle, and related screen motion | transforms on a flat PNG do not create articulated anatomy |
| Sprite sequence | hand-drawn frame-by-frame wing or facial motion | keeps a deliberately illustrated feeling | higher asset/memory cost; use only for a few distinctive sequences |
| Video / WebM / GIF | campaign preview or documentation only | easy sharing and review | not the interactive production character; avoid in ordinary screens |

Current workspace substrate: `lottie-react-native@7.5.0`, `react-native-reanimated@4.7.1`, and `@lottiefiles/dotlottie-react@0.13.5` are present in the mobile manifest. The dotLottie React package is the web player; the native dotLottie player is a separate package. Rive is not currently part of the app dependency graph. No new native animation dependency is assumed by this kit.

**Recommended rollout:** use existing Lottie and Reanimated for finite moments and surrounding motion once the master is approved. Keep Rive as the preferred research candidate for true interactive gaze/gesture, but first run a narrow proof against the exact Expo/RN/Worklets/Nitro matrix and the app's low-memory devices. Rive's October 2026 runtime notes mark React Native 0.5.0 out of beta and document native runtime changes; that makes a version-matched compatibility review necessary before adoption. [Rive runtime updates, October 2026](https://community.rive.app/c/announcements/rive-runtime-updates-october-1-2026)

## Responsive, accessible, and performance rules

- Direct-gaze pose is an authored front-facing expression, not a promise of camera gaze tracking.
- Keep scenes independent of screen copy; layout controls placement and crop at phone, tablet, and desktop widths.
- Make a useful static image for every animated state. Reduced motion removes movement without removing emotional/status meaning.
- Pause loops when off-screen or backgrounded; load one active character scene; keep inline art legible at 24–48 px.
- Keep detailed scene art out of status indicators. Do not make Tutii permanently visible in tabs, settings rows, or dense data surfaces.
- Preserve high contrast for the silhouette and facial read; do not encode success/error solely with color or light.
- Keep animation purpose-led, brief, interruptible, and quiet. No idle glitter, random wandering, prolonged wing flapping, confetti, or repeated attention capture.
- Generate platform-aware crops and formats from one reviewed source; do not stretch a portrait pose to fill a wide web hero.

## Canonical production path

1. Treat the existing mobile art and this concept board as references, not final production art.
2. Build one editable, layered vector master in the design source of truth already established by the mascot pipeline. The model must separate body, head, eyes/eyelids, antennae, wings, abdomen light, limbs, and optional motes/props.
3. Approve face, proportions, light behavior, palette, silhouette, and 48 px recognizability before producing role contexts.
4. Build expressions and poses as components/variants of the same source; compose scene illustrations around them.
5. Export named static and animated assets with source attribution, audience, context, state, theme, scale, format, dimensions, and approval status.
6. Validate every export on vellum, dark ink, and a natural photographic surface; inspect alpha, small-size legibility, cultural representation, and motion fallbacks.
7. Promote only reviewed files to production and connect them through the existing semantic mascot model. Screens continue to request meaning (role/state/theme/scale); they do not import raw filenames.

### Working collection size

The first complete library target is **18 character poses, 10 context families, 24 core scene illustrations, 7 motion clips, and 3 sizes × 2 themes for approved static exports**. Each delivered pose or scene is an individual asset file; pose sheets are review references only. Scene illustrations can cover multiple audiences when that is truthful; they are not multiplied into nearly identical one-role-per-image variants. The two body silhouettes share pose and motion work through swappable anatomy layers. Campaign pieces can expand the catalog without changing Tutii's identity.

## Provenance and originality

Use the existing Tutii artwork only as a continuity reference. Keep prompts, source references, authoring files, and export dates with every generated or drawn piece. Do not reproduce Porkbun, Apache Beam, or another mascot's face, anatomy, pose signature, palette, typography, or art treatment. The goal is useful breadth and consistency, not resemblance to a competitor's collection.

## Current concept artifact

It is **concept-only**: the current render retains too much golden glow and childlike proportion to approve as Tutii's master. ## Acceptance checklist

- Tutii is recognizable from silhouette and face without a halo or context.
- Front-facing welcome looks directly toward the viewer.
- Palette feels natural and Gurudevi-owned; gold remains a small firefly-light accent.
- Adult and child audiences both feel addressed; role meaning comes from situation and gesture, never costume.
- Each scene has a meaningful context and a clear product purpose.
- Static, reduced-motion, light/dark, small-scale, and web/mobile exports are defined.
- Provenance and originality are auditable.
