# Material Reality: Access, Language, Money, and Assumed Infrastructure

## Possibility first

A person can participate without purchasing a new device, mastering English, maintaining continuous data, or becoming the family's technical administrator. Someone with excellent equipment can enjoy richer capabilities without waiting for everyone else to own it. A school can offer shared access and local resources without becoming an unpaid miniature cloud company.

The future should expand what people can do. The present design must meet actual circumstances without turning deprivation into a permanent product identity or assuming the poorest imaginable user represents everyone.

## Constraint dimensions, not fixed personas

| Dimension | Situations to discover | Consequence for design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Device | Feature phone, old smartphone, capable handset, shared phone, school desktop | Distinct reachable doors; installation and compatibility cannot be assumed |
| Ownership/custody | Personal device, parent-controlled device, borrowed/communal access | Session privacy, discreet notifications, logout/account isolation |
| Storage/memory | Low free space, aggressive OS eviction, low RAM, older browser | Small resource packs, bounded caches, honest persistence limits |
| Connectivity | None, intermittent, messaging-only allowance, constrained bandwidth, reliable broadband | No-link essentials, drafts/later sync, optional rich downloads |
| Power | Reliable charging, low battery, outages, no local UPS | Low-energy modes; hub benefits require power/custody assessment |
| Language/literacy | Sinhala, Tamil, English, mixed language, varied reading/typing confidence | Human-validated language; text/audio choice; understandable recovery |
| Time/attention | Busy teacher, parent working shifts, short school periods | Short task paths and no continuous monitoring obligation |
| Money | No budget to buy handset/data/subscription; prepaid variability | Essential access subsidy/funding plan; avoid mandatory purchased enrichment |
| Ability/accessibility | Vision, hearing, motor, cognitive or temporary constraints | Accessible controls and multiple equivalent expressions |
| Trust/social safety | Fear of surveillance, shared-family privacy, school power imbalance | Voluntary private use, minimal disclosure, meaningful correction/refusal |
| Institutional resources | Technical staff absent, limited printers/router/power, donated equipment | Services need explicit owner; ordinary staff cannot inherit invisible IT duties |

These constraints combine differently over time. A technically capable person may still lack time or money today. A low-income family may have a modern shared device. A school can have broadband but no person able to restore a database. Discover the combination for the specific task.

## Grounded contextual sources and their boundary

Sri Lanka's [Department of Census and Statistics computer-literacy bulletin index](https://www.statistics.gov.lk/ComputerLiteracy/StaticalInformation/Bulletins) provides survey evidence, including 2025 publications. It is a starting point for population context, not proof of this pilot's device ownership, English ability, or confidence. No national percentage is used here to assign individuals a persona.

[Dialog's current prepaid plans](https://dialog.lk/mobile/prepaid/plans) advertise application-specific social access. The [TRCSL-approved 2026 social-media tariff](https://www.trc.gov.lk/content/files/tariff/tariff2026/TRC-D-PRO-25-04-New%20Social%20Media%20Plan%20for%20Dialog%20Mobile%20Customers.pdf) demonstrates that application-specific allowances can be bounded. Neither proves a participant has that plan or that external links, media variants, VPN traffic, or bot interactions receive identical treatment. Check the actual package and ordinary user workflow.

Current device affordability, carrier coverage, electricity continuity, language competence and school staffing were not surveyed in this task. Do not present assumed scarcity or abundance as a measured baseline.

## The cost of reaching a feature

Total access cost includes handset purchase/replacement, data provisioning, installation/update, model/resource download, charging, user time, subscription, institutional support, and recovery. A free API or open-source model removes only one possible charge. SMS can reach some basic phones but needs service, credit/account arrangements, safe routing, and readable language; it is not an unlimited free client.

Zero internet means WhatsApp, Telegram, cloud edge, and remote agents cannot presently communicate from that device. A previously provisioned local tool may continue. A feature phone without a usable app/browser needs SMS/voice/physical/institution-assisted paths, whose availability must be built and funded. Gurudevi's current native stack cannot be promised to run on every old phone.

An unprovisioned device cannot obtain its first app/model/resource pack from a disconnected cloud. Plan an assisted provisioning or physical/local distribution path where relevant, with provenance and updates. Physical QR transfer assumes a usable camera, screen/print, nearby participants and trustworthy contents. Paper itself assumes supplies, access, and safe custody.

## Assumed-infrastructure register

For every pilot, explicitly record: who owns handset and SIM; minimum observed OS/browser; free storage; charging; daily usable network windows; messaging allowance versus open data; required language; installation channel; school router/local connectivity; available printer/shared workstation; paid operator; credential/backup custodian; support contact; and fallback after device or staff loss.

Each entry must be **verified**, **provided by the program**, **optional**, or **unresolved**. “School Wi-Fi exists” is insufficient: confirm classroom reach, access credentials, capacity, power, and who repairs it. Donated equipment is not maintained equipment. Available resources at one pilot do not prove availability at another school.

## Essential and enriched paths

For each task, state the least-resource complete path and richer alternatives. Essential information should fit in-channel when browser access is uncertain. Keep action input small and support delayed submission where its domain permits. Provide Sinhala/Tamil/English meaning verified with users, not merely machine-translated labels. Audio is optional because privacy, hearing, noise, and language-model fit vary.

When no digital path fits, a human/physical institutional bridge may be appropriate. It requires named custody and attribution: entering a parent's paper request is staff-mediated work, not evidence the parent authenticated digitally. The authorization and accountability must represent what actually occurred.

## Hostile access review

If a person must buy an upgraded phone to obtain an essential affordance, enrichment has become exclusion. If every fallback requires a trained volunteer who is never available, it is imaginary continuity. If a local hub saves bandwidth but requires the principal to patch servers, burden has moved. If users cannot understand an error in their language, technically successful delivery is unusable. Conversely, if poverty assumptions prevent offering capable users better tools, the product has constrained agency unnecessarily.

## Integration round R12

Add an access/prerequisite/total-cost dimension to R11. A capability can be globally available yet locally unreachable. Preserve a well-defined essential path, optional enrichment, and explicitly funded assistance where required. Treat uncertainty as a research question. No future milestone can assume users naturally upgrade, gain English, acquire money, or become operators.
