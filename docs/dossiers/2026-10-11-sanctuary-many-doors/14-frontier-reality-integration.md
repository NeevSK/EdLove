# Frontier × Reality: Capability Placement, Conditions, and Future Integration

## Integration round R13: a capability is an intersection

Useful affordance = technical availability ∩ reachable resources ∩ understandable interaction ∩ legitimate authority ∩ affordable operation ∩ useful human outcome. This is a reasoning checklist, not a numerical model. A failure in one condition can make a sophisticated capability useless for a particular person while leaving it valuable elsewhere.

Dream broadly, then explore multiple ways to realize the same human outcome. Do not choose one future technology and force every participant into its prerequisites. Do not choose one scarcity persona and suppress every richer path.

## Cross-seam decisions

| Human outcome | Frontier option | Real prerequisite | Essential/alternate expression | Experiment that earns expansion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Save a lesson draft | On-device speech/inference | Supported model/language, storage, battery, initial download | Text/manual draft; optional server voice when affordable | Accuracy + correction time + energy/data on representative devices |
| Read class materials offline | Phone/browser cache or school hub | Prior provisioning, storage/local connection, content rights | Small text pack, paper/shared access | Open after restart/offline, eviction behavior, local-network field test |

| Routine timetable question | Device assistant/product MCP | Compatible assistant, delegated read scope, current or dated cache | Mobile/web lookup or short familiar-channel query | One actual assistant/device with expiry and minimum disclosure |
| Classroom work through regional outage | Local hub or device queue | Power, maintained hub or provisioned device, bounded offline rules | Draft/cached work plus later submission | Partition and rejoin drill with actual operator and participant |

| Low-latency protected action | Regional/provider edge | Network access, compatible runtime, DB/authority proximity | Central commit with honest pending state | End-to-end latency and failure proof; edge hop alone is insufficient |

## Placement policy for a bounded first experiment

Begin with a human outcome, effect class, resource envelope, privacy preference, and evidence. Prefer an existing deterministic/manual path when it completes the task. Add optional inference or alternate placement where it demonstrably reduces work. Keep authoritative protected commits at their validated owner until a different authority model is deliberately designed.

Do not silently route private material to cloud because the device cannot compute it. Explain the required disclosure and offer a manual/local route. Do not silently use an expensive backup channel. Ask for the user's channel/cost preference when relevant; record institutional funding for essential notice escalation.

Progressive capability detection should ask what a runtime can do, not infer a user's social class. Device tier, account/role, network state, and preference are separate facts. “Advanced mode” must not grant stronger school rights, and low-resource mode must not weaken authorization.

## Measurement envelopes before performance promises

For each experiment, record cold installation/resource download size, steady-state bytes per completed task, peak memory, storage growth, battery/thermal behavior, startup and correction time, language accuracy, offline duration, and recovery. Select actual devices from observed participants. An emulator benchmark cannot establish old-handset performance; a flagship does not establish local population reach.

Numeric budgets should follow observation. Until then, use declared experimental targets, not invented supported minima. Compare total completed-task cost and participant burden across local model, cloud model, structured form, and human-assisted path. A faster model that requires repeated correction loses the comparison.

## Future trajectories under different realities

**Capability growth, access stable:** optional local AI improves for current compatible users; essential manual/channel access continues. Measure whether enrichment adds an unfair institutional requirement.

**Device improvement, money/connectivity still constrained:** richer offline deterministic tools or local models may help after provisioning; download/update cost still matters. Explore community resource distribution rather than assuming open-data abundance.

**Connectivity improves, devices remain old:** small server-rendered/web or familiar-channel actions may outperform local inference. The cloud still needs affordable service and trustworthy disclosure.

**School capacity grows:** a maintained hub/cell may become viable. Operator training, power, backup and key custody are capabilities themselves. Reassess rather than permanently rejecting institutional independence.

**Resources worsen:** data prices, power, funding, or support disappear. Prioritize usable exports, cached selected work, low-byte notices and human continuity. The product must be able to contract gracefully.

**New interface emerges:** connect it to validated read/draft/command contracts with compatible consent and receipts. Preserve discoverability and correction; do not mistake novelty for value.

## Milestone additions

At 0–2 weeks, add participant resource and assumed-infrastructure inventory to discovery. At 2–6 weeks, prove the minimum useful task on the actual resource envelope. At 6–12 weeks, test one optional enriched placement and compare net effort. At 3–6 months, record participant cost and institution support load alongside pilot benefits. At 6–18 months, reassess maturity/support quarterly before selecting additional platforms. At 18–36 months, test hubs/cells only with a funded operator. Across 3–10 years, review access distribution and data portability as interfaces evolve; user upgrades remain a possibility, not a delivery dependency.

## Final integrated judgment

The architecture should support growing capability without assuming growing resources for everyone. Its essential work must have declared prerequisites and recovery. Its future experiments can be ambitious precisely because they have clear evidence boundaries and do not pretend every participant already has the required hardware, language, money, connection, or support.

The “bedrock” is therefore both semantic and material: trustworthy action/record meaning, and the people/devices/resources that can sustain it. Technical discovery and field discovery remain coupled throughout the roadmap. Neither is a one-time feasibility paragraph.
