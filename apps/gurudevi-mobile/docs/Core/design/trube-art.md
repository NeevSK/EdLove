# Trübe-Art
## A Living Light Rendering Library
### Architecture v0.3 — 2026 Production Ready

> "Colors are the deeds and sufferings of light."  
> — Johann Wolfgang von Goethe, *Theory of Colors*, 1810

## Preamble: The 2026 Mandate

Every rendering system built before this one made the same quiet mistake.  
They treated color as data. A value to be stored, a property to be applied, a number to be computed. They were built by engineers thinking in hexadecimal, not by people who have stood beneath an evening sky and felt something move inside them.

The result is a world of screens that are technically accurate and spiritually dead.

**Trübe-Art v0.3** begins from a different question entirely:  
*What if light on a screen could behave the way light behaves in the world — absorbed, scattered, held, released — passing through living material rather than sitting on top of dead surfaces?*

The name comes from Goethe's concept of *Trübe* — the turbid medium. The slight haziness through which light passes and becomes color. Not the light source. Not the surface itself. But the living space between them where color is born.

This is that space, made renderable. This version commits to the **2026 Vision**: prioritizing perceptual richness over legacy compatibility, leveraging API 31+ for unbeatable fidelity, and establishing Android 10 (API 29) as the hard floor for a future-proof experience.

## I. The Philosophy of Living Color

### 1.1 Color Is Not a Property
In conventional rendering, color is assigned. A button is `#3B82F6`. A background is `rgba(255,255,255,0.8)`. These are not colors. They are addresses.

Real color is not assigned — it is experienced. It emerges from the relationship between light, material, depth, and time. The green of a young leaf is not a hex value. It is:
*   Chlorophyll absorbing red and blue, releasing green
*   Sunlight passing through the leaf, not merely reflecting off it
*   The slightly cooler color of the shadow underneath
*   The golden rim where direct light catches the translucent edge
*   The way it shifts when a cloud passes

**Trübe-Art renders the relationship, not the value.**

### 1.2 The Turbid Medium as Metaphor and Mechanism
Goethe observed that color as we experience it is never pure. It always passes through something — atmosphere, water, glass, biological tissue. This medium scatters, absorbs, and transforms light before it reaches the eye.

This is not a flaw. This is where beauty lives.

Trübe-Art treats every rendered surface as a turbid medium. Light enters it, moves through it, and emerges changed. The degree of turbidity, the internal variation of the medium, the direction of light — these together produce what we experience as **aliveness**.

### 1.3 Imperfection as Truth
Mathematical perfection is visually dead.
*   A perfect gradient has no soul.
*   A perfectly periodic animation reveals its mechanism.
*   A perfectly uniform surface offers nothing for the eye to rest in.

Living things are imperfect in precise ways. Aurora shifts but never repeats. Silk has micro-variations in weave. Mist has no edges. Trübe-Art encodes imperfection as a design primitive, not an accident to be corrected.

### 1.4 The Kells Principle
The animated film *The Secret of Kells* (2009) represents a particular quality of visual intentionality that serves as a guiding aesthetic for this library.

In that film, every frame feels as though someone cared about every pixel. Color pools in corners with intention. Gold glows from within. The geometry is mathematically impossible and emotionally true.

**The Kells Principle states:** Every surface rendered by Trübe-Art should feel as though it was made with care, not generated by formula.

This is achieved through:
*   Micro-variation at the pixel level that prevents any region from feeling stamped.
*   Color that shifts based on depth and angle, not just position.
*   Edges that are uncertain, as edges in nature always are.
*   Light that has personality — direction, warmth, history.

## III. Architecture v0.3 — The 2026 Production Stack

### 3.1 The Strategic Shift: Future-First
Version 0.2 was architecturally sound but too accommodating of legacy hardware. Version 0.3 makes a decisive pivot: **We optimize for the future.**

*   **Hard Floor:** Android 10 (API 29). Devices below this are outside the target demographic for a premium visual experience.
*   **Optimal Target:** Android 12 (API 31)+. This unlocks `RenderEffect`, Skia Graphite, and full Vulkan stability.
*   **Legacy Strategy:** Android 5-9 are no longer primary tiers. They are served via a **Compatibility Mode** (static assets or simplified shaders) to avoid dragging down the core architecture.

### 3.2 The Revised Three-Layer Architecture

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 LANGUAGE LAYER                   │
│                                                  │
│  surface("aurora") · surface("young-leaf")       │
│  Feeling-first API · No hex codes at surface     │
│  Curated surface library · Builder's eye         │
│                                                  │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                  TIME LAYER                      │
│                                                  │
│  Reanimated useFrameCallback on UI thread        │
│  WebGPU pipeline objects — pre-warmed once       │
│  Temporal scheduler — surface-aware skip rates   │
│  Adaptive resolution — thermal-aware             │
│  Entropy signal — prevents perfect loops         │
│                                                  │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                  SOUL LAYER                      │
│                                                  │
│  TSL shaders — write once, run everywhere        │
│  ├─ WebGPU/WGSL path (API 31+ / Vulkan)         │
│  └─ Skia SkRuntimeEffect/GLSL (API 29-30)       │
│                                                  │
│  Simplex noise · fBm · Domain warp · Flow noise  │
│  Subsurface scattering · Rayleigh approximation  │
│  Micro-variation · Chromatic complement shadow   │
│                                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### The Soul Layer
Pure GPU computation. TSL shaders that compile to WGSL for WebGPU and GLSL for Skia automatically. This is where the Kells Principle lives — pixel-level intentionality encoded in math that runs in parallel across every pixel simultaneously.

**Critical Rule:** Soul layer shaders must be **pre-compiled and pipeline-cached at app startup**, never per-frame. This is non-negotiable for frame budget compliance.

#### The Time Layer
Built on `useFrameCallback` from Reanimated 4, running as a worklet on the UI thread. It never touches the JavaScript thread during rendering. It watches the frame budget and makes decisions every frame:
*   Which surfaces need full shader recomputation?
*   Which can interpolate from last frame?
*   Is the device thermally throttling? Drop resolution.

#### The Language Layer
The developer-facing API. It speaks in feelings. It translates `surface("evening-sky", { time: "golden-hour" })` into shader parameters and scheduling instructions. The translation is curated by the library — it is not algorithmic. This is where human taste is encoded as code.

### 3.3 The Tiered Device Strategy (2026 Focus)

| Tier | OS Version | Path | Quality | Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tier 1 (Primary)** | **API 31+ (Android 12+)** | **RN WebGPU → Vulkan** | **Full Fidelity** | Unbeatable visual experience. All effects enabled. `RenderEffect` for blurs. |
| **Tier 2 (Standard)** | **API 29-30 (Android 10-11)** | **Skia Graphite → Vulkan/GL** | **High Fidelity** | Full Trübe quality. Slightly tighter budget. No `RenderEffect` blurs (shader-based). |
| **Tier 3 (Compatibility)** | **API 28 and below** | **Static/Pre-baked** | **Graceful Degradation** | No live shaders. Pre-rendered textures. Beautiful but static. |
| **Tier 4 (Thermal)** | **Any (Throttling)** | **Current Tier @ 25%** | **Resilient** | Resolution drop + Temporal skip. Auto-recovery. |

**Note:** We do not apologize for Tier 3. It is a design decision to preserve the integrity of Tier 1 & 2. Users on legacy devices see a beautiful static surface, not a broken shader.

## IV. The Frame Budget — Honest Numbers

### 4.1 Revised Budget with Real Stack (API 31+)

**FRAME BUDGET — TIER 1 (WebGPU / Vulkan)**

| Operation | Cost | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Pipeline submission (pre-warmed) | 0.1ms | Zero allocation |
| Shader execution (50% resolution) | 1.5–3ms | Simplex, fBm, Domain Warp |
| Upscale + composite over UI | 0.5–1ms | GPU composite |
| Reanimated worklet overhead | 0.2ms | UI Thread |
| UI thread render (native UI) | 4–6ms | React Native |
| Buffer (GC, thermal, system) | 3–5ms | Safety margin |
| **TOTAL** | **~10–15ms** | **✓ 60FPS Target** |

**FRAME BUDGET — TIER 2 (Skia / OpenGL ES)**

| Operation | Cost | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| SkRuntimeEffect shader execution | 2–4ms | Slightly higher overhead |
| Skia composite | 1–2ms | CPU/GPU hybrid |
| UI thread render | 4–6ms | React Native |
| Buffer | 4–6ms | Safety margin |
| **TOTAL** | **~13–18ms** | **✓ Viable** |

### 4.2 The Critical Pre-warm Rule
GPU pipeline objects — shader programs, bind group layouts, render pipelines — must be compiled **once at startup**, never per frame. Per-frame pipeline creation is the single most common cause of shader jank on mobile.

*   **Startup Cost:** ~200-400ms one-time.
*   **Per-frame Cost:** Near zero for pipeline state.

### 4.3 Temporal Scheduling
Not every surface needs 60FPS. We trade raw framerate for perceptual smoothness.

| Surface | Update Rate | Interpolated | Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Aurora | Every 5f | Yes | Slow atmospheric drift |
| Mist | Every 4f | Yes | Gentle volumetric shift |
| Evening Sky | Every 7f | Yes | Very slow color decay |
| Young Leaf | Every 3f | Yes | Subtle breathing |
| Silk | Every 2f | Yes | Micro-shimmer |
| Water (flow) | Every frame | No | Motion requires frequency |

**Between updates:** GPU-cost-free linear interpolation between last and next computed state. Imperceptible to human vision.

## V. The Noise Vocabulary — Technical Specification

Organic visual quality emerges from layered noise. Trübe-Art uses a specific, GPU-efficient vocabulary. All noise functions are purely computational — no texture lookups.

1.  **Simplex Noise (Primary):** Faster than Perlin. No directional artifacts. Used for base turbidity.
2.  **Fractal Brownian Motion (fBm):** Layered octaves. Creates self-similar complexity (clouds, water).
3.  **Domain-Warped Noise:** Input coordinates displaced by noise. Creates folding, fluid quality.
4.  **psrdnoise:** Periodic, smoothly rotatable noise. Used for anisotropic reflection (Silk) and normals.
5.  **Flow Noise:** Noise evolved along a vector field. Used for waterfalls and flowing silk.
6.  **Meta-Noise (Entropy Signal):** A very-low-frequency noise (47+ second period) that modulates seeds. Prevents looping. Essential for aliveness.

## VI. Color Theory — The Goethe Implementation

### 6.1 Color Temperature as Primary Variable
Hue shifts are noticed. Temperature shifts are felt.
Trübe-Art surfaces shift primarily in color temperature (2700K - 12000K). This matches how natural light behaves. Shadows carry the chromatic complement of the light source (Goethe's observation).

### 6.2 Internal Color Variation
Every Trübe surface contains:
*   **Subsurface warmth:** Where light is absorbed then re-emitted.
*   **Self-shadow cool:** Micro-shadow areas.
*   **Edge chromatic shift:** Temperature changes at thin edges.
*   **Depth pigment bleed:** Deep color affecting surface appearance.

### 6.3 Avoiding the Gradient Trap
A gradient is two colors holding hands. That is not what Trübe-Art renders.
Trübe-Art renders color that has memory of what it came from. The result looks nothing like a gradient even when the underlying color range is narrow. It has dimension.

## VII. The API

### 7.1 The Feeling-First Language
```typescript
// What Trübe-Art sounds like:
surface("aurora")
surface("young-leaf", { light: "morning" })
surface("mist", { density: "heavy", lightSource: "warm" })
surface("evening-sky", { time: "golden-hour" })

// What it does not sound like:
shader({
  noiseOctaves: 6,
  frequency: 0.003,
  colorA: "#4ECDC4",
  colorB: "#44CF6C"
})
```

### 7.2 React Native Integration
```typescript
import { TrubeCanvas, surface } from 'trube-art';

// As a background
<TrubeCanvas 
  surface={surface("aurora")} 
  style={StyleSheet.absoluteFill}
/>

// As a UI element surface
<TrubeView surface={surface("silk", { motion: "gentle" })}>
  <Button title="Press me" />
</TrubeView>
```
All rendering runs on the UI thread via Reanimated worklet. Zero JS thread involvement during frame rendering.

### 7.3 Composability
Trübe surfaces never replace UI. They breathe beneath it. Native touch handling, accessibility, and UI behavior are entirely unaffected.

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Native UI Layer                       │  Crisp, interactive
│  (buttons, text, cards, gestures)      │  Full React Native
├────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Alpha Composition                     │  GPU composite
├────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Trübe Surface Layer                   │  Living, breathing
│  (aurora, silk, mist, waterfall...)    │  Soul Layer shaders
└────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

*   **Shader execution ceiling:** 4ms (Tier 1), 5ms (Tier 2)

*   **Per-frame pipeline cost:** Near zero (pre-warmed)
*   **Minimum temporal skip:** Every 2 frames
*   **Maximum temporal skip:** Every 7 frames

*   **Interpolation between updates:** Always — zero GPU cost

## IX. The Extended Canvas — Motifs, Ornaments, and Scores

As Trübe-Art evolved from a rendering engine into a complete **Design Language**, three new systems were added to provide structural and symbolic depth on top of the living light surfaces.

### 9.1 The Motif System (The Sacred Hand)
Motifs are procedural, vector-like sketches that mimic human draftsmanship. They represent the "Kells Principle" of intentional, hand-authored form.
*   **Lotus Sketch:** A central focal geometry for spiritual clarity.
*   **Branch Flow:** Directional, organic structure for peripheral air.
*   **Tide Lines:** Meditative, horizontal flow representing memory and transition.

**Implementation:** Motifs use randomized jitter and variable line weight to ensure no two renders are identical. They come in three styles: `draft`, `ink`, and `luminous`.

### 9.2 The Ornament System (The Micro-Detail)
Ornaments provide a layer of fine-grained structural "noise" that grounds the ethereal surfaces.
*   **Filigree:** Delicate, vine-like connections.
*   **Constellation:** A scattered field of points and light edges.
*   **Vein:** Subsurface structural lines that mimic organic tissue.

### 9.3 The Score Composer (The Performance)
A **Score** is a canonical scene definition. It orchestates the entire Trübe stack into a single, authored performance. 
A Score defines:
1.  **A Stage:** A layered stack of Surfaces (`body`, `air`, `shimmer`).
2.  **A Motif:** The symbolic focal point.
3.  **An Ornament:** The structural micro-detail.
4.  **A Rationale:** The spiritual and functional intent of the scene.

**Canonical Examples:**
*   `lotus-dawn`: Quiet awakening (Young Leaf + Mist + Lotus).
*   `goethe-night-study`: Ambient depth (Aurora + Mist + Constellation).
*   `lotus-vigil`: Ceremonial stillness (Aurora + Mist + Constellation).
