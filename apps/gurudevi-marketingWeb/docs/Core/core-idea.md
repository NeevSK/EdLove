## Epistemic Truth: A Systemic View of Education

This section attempts to understand the education ecosystem through a systemic and ecological lens, drawing a parallel to Bronfenbrenner’s ecological map. Education is viewed as a polycentric, cybernetic system, though its complexity is often oversimplified.
At its core, learning is relational—it does not happen in isolation. This relationship can be best understood as a process of "impedance matching." We propose the term “teaning” (a fusion of "teaching" and "learning") to name this relational site of education. Anyone involved can only support this relationship to flourish or facilitate this impedance match; given the autopoietic (self-creating) nature of human beings, external intervention can only serve to perturb the system.
Historically, education involved learning through direct life experience ("swimming in the sea of life"). However, with the rise of organized, institutional education, our implicit model has become institutional. Within this institutional context, the system often functions top-down, with broken cybernetic feedback loops, despite the reality of a polycentric system. In contexts like Sri Lanka, the outcome of the educational process aligns with the principle of POSIWID ("The purpose of a system is what it does"), even as its stated ideology aims for different results.
Furthermore, future-oriented learning appears to be primarily a middle-class value. If one has limited surplus resources, the focus is on survival rather than long-term, strategic future planning, which is where institutional education often positions itself. Pushing this surplus-dependent educational model to the general population without adequate resource support leads to a disconnect between policy aspirations and the reality of POSIWID.
In the classroom, the actual educational experience is a complex "dance" of impedance matching, co-regulation, energy, and affective management, alongside content delivery. This is often at odds with the dominant ideology, which reduces education to merely content and exams—a situation driven by factors like Goodhart's law and a lack of resources and capabilities. This complex, nuanced understanding forms the simple essence of this analysis.
