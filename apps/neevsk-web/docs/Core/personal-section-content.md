## ABOUT / INTRO

### Version 1: Phenomenological (Warm, Felt, Story-Based)

I grew up in two worlds at once.

The first world was Pannala — a small town in Sri Lanka where my parents worked government jobs, where education was the only path forward, where I learned early that stability meant safety.

The second world was everywhere else. It lived in the library books I devoured from age 10, one per week, sometimes more. It lived in the films I watched during COVID lockdowns — four, five in a day — until the boundaries between my life and the stories blurred. Philosophy, psychology, existentialism. Lives I'd never live, but could feel completely.

I think I became more comfortable in that second world. The symbolic one. The one made of ideas and patterns and systems. While other kids learned through doing, I learned through reading. While they played, I thought. It made me... different. Detached, maybe. Or just attuned to different frequencies.

By the time I left my hometown for Nalanda College in Colombo at age 11, living in hostels and boarding houses, the separation didn't feel like separation anymore. It felt normal. I carried my real home with me — in books, in thoughts, in the systems I was beginning to see everywhere.

That's when I started calling myself an existential cyberneticist. Not because it sounds impressive (it doesn't, really), but because it's true. I exist in the space between human experience and systems thinking. Between feeling everything deeply (INFJ, for those who speak that language) and seeing the patterns that connect everything (cybernetics, for those who speak mine).

I studied Business Analytics at University of Moratuwa because it seemed practical. But what I'm actually building is something else entirely — sanctuaries. Spaces where teachers can breathe. Ecosystems where education transforms not through force, but through care. Systems that grow without needing me to hold them together.

I left a stable IT job to do this. Not because I'm brave, but because I couldn't not do it. When you grow up reading philosophy and watching films about what it means to be human, eventually you have to ask: what does it mean for ME to be human? And for me, it means building the soul behind the machines.

This is the personal part. The part that doesn't go in CVs. The part that explains why I think the way I think, build the way I build, and care the way I care.

## JOURNEY (Timeline Format)

### Age 10 — The Library Card
Registered at Pannala public library. Started reading one book a week. This is when the symbolic world opened up. Philosophy. Psychology. Stories about human life I'd never lived but could feel completely.

### Age 11 — Nalanda College & Hostel Life  
Passed scholarship exam. Left home for Colombo. Lived in hostels and boarding houses through high school. The separation didn't feel like separation — I'd already learned to carry my real home (books, ideas, systems) with me.

### Age 18-20 — COVID & The Film Marathon
Lockdowns. Sometimes 4-5 films a day. My worldview crystallized through cinema — the existential, the philosophical, the human. I was forming my lens.

### Age 20 — The IT Startup Year
Worked at Axcer Innovations as management trainee. Played parts of scrum master, finance, HR, QA, BA. Learned how systems work (and don't work) in practice. Left after a year — couldn't ignore the pull toward something more meaningful.

### Age 21 — University of Moratuwa
Studying Business Analytics, but really studying how to build systems that serve humans, not the other way around. CGPA 3.3, but that's not the real education.

### Age 21-22 — Systems Thinking Shift
Transition from heavy critical theory to grounded systemic view. Discovered cybernetics, enactivism, systems thinking through physics. Everything clicked. This is how I see now.

### Age 22 — Vice President, Student Union
Leadership not through authority, but through seeing patterns others miss. Building student initiatives. Learning what it means to serve.

### Age 23 — Building Gurudevi
The sanctuary project begins. Teacher wellbeing for Sri Lanka. EdLove ecosystem. Intuitui Labs. This is where everything converges — the books, the films, the systems thinking, the care for education. Building the soul behind the machines.
