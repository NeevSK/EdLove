## PURPOSE

The Epistemic Core is the intellectual backbone of edlove. It is where knowledge lives — deep, honest, interconnected knowledge about education.

It is **sovereign**: it has its own space, its own identity, its own reason to exist. You can come here without visiting any other domain and find profound value.

AND it connects to everything. Every other domain draws from it. Every paradigm in Domain I has a deep-dive here. Every gap in Domain II has research here. Every feature in Gurudevi is grounded in frameworks here. The Sangha's wisdom flows into it. The Resource ecosystem's needs are informed by it.

**It is the skeleton that holds the body. Sovereign and essential.**

When someone arrives here, they should feel: *"This is where the real thinking lives. This is where I come to understand — not just what education is, but WHY it is the way it is, and what it could become."*

## THE FOUR PILLARS OF THE EPISTEMIC CORE

### Pillar 1: Thinkers
**Profiles of the people who changed how we think about education.**

Not just biographies. Not just "famous educators." These are deep explorations of their THINKING — their philosophy, their context, their revolutionary insights, their legacy.

**Phase 1 Thinkers (5 profiles):**

| Thinker | Why They Matter |
|---------|----------------|
| **Paulo Freire** | Critical pedagogy. Education as liberation. "Banking" model critique. |
| **Maria Montessori** | The prepared environment. Child as author of their own learning. |
| **Ivan Illich** | Deschooling Society. The radical critique of schooling as an institution. |
| **bell hooks** | Teaching as transgressive act. Engaged pedagogy. Education as the practice of freedom. |
| **[Indigenous educator/elder]** | To be researched. Must be told BY indigenous voices. Ancient wisdom of place-based learning. |

**Each profile includes:**
1. Opening quote (their most powerful single idea)
2. Context (who they were, when, where, what they faced)
3. Core philosophy (3–4 key concepts, explained simply)
4. Revolutionary insight (what made them genuinely different)
5. Legacy and application (how their ideas live today)
6. Resources (books, videos, further exploration)
7. Reflection questions (for the reader to sit with)

### Pillar 2: Theoretical Frameworks
**The intellectual models that help us understand education as a system.**

These are not abstractions for academics. They are **lenses** — ways of seeing that make the invisible visible.

**Phase 1 Frameworks (3 core + 2 to be specified):**

| Framework | What It Reveals |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Devneevma Pressure Theory** | The systemic pressures acting on education — how they compound, how they shape teacher and student behavior, how to map and respond to them. *(Neev's framework — needs full articulation)* |
| **Education as Ecological System** | Education is not a machine. It is an ecology — living, interdependent, evolving. Understanding it as ecology changes how we intervene in it. |
| **Thermodynamic Semiotics in Learning** | How meaning (semiotics) flows through educational systems like energy flows through physical ones. Entropy, order, transformation. |
| **[Framework 4]** | To be specified by Neev |
| **[Framework 5]** | To be specified by Neev |

**Each framework includes:**
1. The core idea (explained simply — no jargon)
2. Why it matters (what it lets you see that you couldn't before)
3. The model (visual diagram or metaphor)
4. Application (how to actually USE this lens in practice)
5. Connections to other frameworks
6. Further reading

### Pillar 3: Paradigm Deep-Dives
**Extended explorations of each educational paradigm — beyond what Domain I shows.**

Domain I gives you the landscape. The Epistemic Core gives you the depth.

**Phase 1 Guides (5 paradigm deep-dives):**

| Guide | What It Covers |
|-------|----------------|
| **Montessori: Philosophy & Practice** | The full philosophical foundation + what it looks like in real classrooms |
| **Waldorf: Developmental Approach** | Rudolf Steiner's vision of education following child development stages |
| **Democratic Schools: Student Governance** | How schools actually run when students have real power |
| **Unschooling: Self-Directed Learning** | The philosophy, the practice, the evidence, the real stories |
| **Forest Schools: Nature-Based Education** | Learning through relationship with the natural world |

**Each guide includes:**
1. Essence (one powerful paragraph)
2. Origin story (how and why this emerged)
3. Key principles (the philosophical core)
4. In practice (what it actually LOOKS like — concrete, vivid)
5. Strengths and challenges (honest, not promotional)
6. Real examples (actual schools and projects)
7. How to explore further (resources, communities, next steps)

### Pillar 4: Stories from the Field
**Real human narratives. The lived experience that gives everything else meaning.**

Stories are not illustrations. They are **evidence of a different kind** — the kind that lives in the body, not just the mind.

**Phase 1 Stories (5 narratives):**

| Story Type | What It Shows |
|------------|---------------|
| **Teacher Transformation** | A teacher who shifted from industrial model to something else. What they felt, what they risked, what changed. |
| **Student Journey** | A young person who experienced education differently and what it meant to them. |
| **School Innovation** | A school that tried something new. How it happened, what worked, what didn't. |
| **Family Experience** | A family that made a different educational choice. The process, the fears, the discoveries. |
| **Community Initiative** | A community that created its own learning space. How, why, what it became. |

**Each story includes:**
1. The person's own voice (written in first person, or close to it)
2. Context (where, when, what the situation was)
3. The turning point (what changed and why)
4. The reality (not just the beautiful parts — the hard parts too)
5. What it means now (where things stand today)
6. Reflection (what this story invites us to consider)

## THE LIBRARY INTERFACE

### Browse View (`/library`)
The main library page. Users can:
- **Browse by category**: Thinkers, Frameworks, Guides, Stories
- **Browse by tag**: Montessori, Indigenous, Sri Lanka, Policy, etc.
- **Search**: Full-text search across all content (Pagefind)
- **Filter by domain connection**: "Show me everything related to Domain I"

### Individual Content Pages
Each piece of content has its own page with:
- Generous reading layout (Article layout from 03_TECH)
- Beautiful typography (Libre Baskerville for headings, Inter for body)
- Tags shown prominently
- "Related content" section at the bottom (interconnected links)
- "Connected domains" — which other parts of edlove this relates to
- Reading time estimate

### The Interconnection Web
**This is what makes the Epistemic Core special**: Nothing is an island.

- A thinker profile links to the paradigm deep-dives they inspired
- A framework links to the stories that illustrate it
- A story links to the thinker whose ideas the person was living
- A paradigm deep-dive links to the research that supports it

**The reader can wander.** One piece leads naturally to the next. Like a conversation that deepens.

## CONTENT GENERATION WORKFLOW

All Epistemic Core content is generated through our AI partnership:

### For Thinker Profiles:
```
1. Neev confirms the thinker and what matters about them
2. Claude researches and drafts the full profile
3. Neev reviews, edits, adds personal insight
4. Published
```

### For Frameworks:
```
1. Neev explains the framework (especially Devneevma — this is original)
2. Claude shapes it into accessible prose with visual model suggestions
3. Neev reviews and refines
4. Published
```

### For Guides:
```
1. Claude drafts the full paradigm guide (research-based)
2. Neev reviews for accuracy and adds Sri Lankan context where relevant
3. Published
```

### For Stories:
```
1. Stories ideally come from REAL people (community-contributed)
2. If not available at launch: Claude creates composite stories based on real patterns
3. Clearly labeled: "Based on real experiences" vs. "Shared by [name]"
4. Published
```
