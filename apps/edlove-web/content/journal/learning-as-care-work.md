Education is often described as the delivery of knowledge. That description is
too small for the work people actually do.

Learning depends on attention, trust, patience, safety, and the quiet labour of
people who make room for another person to become more capable. A teacher does
not only transfer information. A family does not only monitor attendance. A
community does not only provide context. Together, they hold a living ecology
around learning.

To call education care work is not to make it softer or less rigorous. It is to
make the real conditions of learning visible. Care asks whether people have the
energy to participate, whether their dignity is protected, and whether the
structures around them help or exhaust them.

This is one of the questions EdLove will keep returning to: how might our
schools and technologies give energy back to the relationships that make
learning possible?
