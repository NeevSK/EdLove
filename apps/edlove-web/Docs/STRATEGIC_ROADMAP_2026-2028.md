## STRATEGIC PRIORITIES ACROSS THREE YEARS

### Priority 1: Stay True to Philosophy

**The Invariants Must Not Change**:
- Education as care work (always the filter)
- Professional yet soulful and alive (the aesthetic)
- Global + Local simultaneously (the dual zoom)
- Nine topological invariants (the constitution)

**Guardrails**:
- Annual philosophy audit against 01_PHILOSOPHY
- Community referendum on any proposed changes to invariants
- Transparent documentation of all major decisions
- Guiding questions asked for every significant choice

### Priority 2: Community as Center

**The Sangha Is Not Optional**:
- Community health metrics as important as growth metrics
- Moderation as care work (not just rule enforcement)
- Community co-creation (not just consumption)
- Trust and safety paramount

**Investments**:
- Community coordinator (Year 2)
- Advanced moderation tools and training
- Regular community pulse checks (surveys, interviews)
- Hub support and coordination resources
- Conflict resolution and healing processes

### Priority 3: Quality Over Quantity

**Content Depth**:
- Every piece meets high bar (edlove voice, thorough, beautiful)
- Better 100 great pieces than 500 mediocre ones
- AI-assisted but human-curated always
- Community contributions reviewed carefully
- Regular content audits and updates

**Platform Experience**:
- Performance never sacrificed (Lighthouse 90+ always)
- Accessibility non-negotiable (WCAG AAA goal)
- Mobile-first (smartphone more accessible than computer)
- Beauty as philosophy (not just decoration)
- Aliveness maintained (site breathes and responds)

### Priority 4: Transparent Sustainability

**Financial Openness**:
- Quarterly financial reports (all income, all expenses)
- No hidden costs or surprises
- Donor intent honored scrupulously
- Resource matches tracked and published
- Impact assessment honest (failures included)

**Funding Diversity**:
- Never >40% from single source
- Multiple revenue streams by Year 2
- Endowment building started Year 3
- Earned revenue ethical and values-aligned
- Free/accessible commitment never compromised

### Priority 5: Research Rigor

**Evidence-Based**:
- All claims sourced and attributed
- Uncertainty acknowledged explicitly
- Research methods transparent
- Data collected ethically (consent, privacy)
- Academic partnerships for credibility

**Knowledge Commons**:
- Open access always (CC BY-SA 4.0)
- Data shared (with privacy protections)
- Methods documented for replication
- Failures published (not just successes)
- Contributing to global knowledge base

## RISK MANAGEMENT STRATEGY

### Risk 1: Mission Drift

**Symptoms**:
- Chasing funding that requires compromise
- Feature creep beyond core purpose
- Aesthetic degradation (corporate-ification)
- Community culture shifts (toxic dynamics)

**Mitigation**:
- Annual philosophy audit
- Community governance participation
- Funding only from values-aligned sources
- Regular check against guiding questions
- Board/advisory with mission as primary concern

### Risk 2: Unsustainable Growth

**Symptoms**:
- Community grows faster than moderation capacity
- Content quality drops to meet volume targets
- Technical infrastructure overwhelmed
- Founder/team burnout

**Mitigation**:
- Growth intentionally paced (not maximized)
- Community closed temporarily if needed
- Content production sustainable (not heroic)
- Infrastructure investment ahead of scale
- Team wellbeing as core metric

### Risk 3: Technological Obsolescence

**Symptoms**:
- Platform feels dated or slow
- Competitor platforms more attractive
- Mobile experience degrades
- Security vulnerabilities accumulate

**Mitigation**:
- Technology refresh every 2-3 years
- Modular architecture (easy to update parts)
- Modern frameworks that are stable (Astro, Svelte)
- Security audits annually
- Accessibility testing continuously

### Risk 4: Financial Instability

**Symptoms**:
- Unable to pay staff/expenses
- Must compromise values for funding
- Emergency appeals to community
- Services shut down

**Mitigation**:
- Multiple revenue streams by Year 2
- 6-month reserve fund by Year 3
- Endowment building started early
- Scalable down (can operate on minimal if needed)
- Transparent financial planning with community

### Risk 5: Reputational Damage

**Symptoms**:
- Public scandal or controversy
- Community loses trust
- Partnerships withdraw
- Media criticism damages credibility

**Mitigation**:
- Transparent operations always
- Quick, honest response to issues
- Community voice in major decisions
- Legal/PR advisory available
- Crisis communication plan prepared

### Risk 6: Founder Dependence

**Symptoms**:
- Platform collapses if Neev unavailable
- No succession plan
- Institutional knowledge siloed
- Community dependent on one person

**Mitigation**:
- Documentation comprehensive (everything written down)
- Co-leadership developed (Year 2)
- Board/advisory with authority
- Community ownership fostered
- Succession plan by Year 3

## DECISION-MAKING FRAMEWORK

### For Strategic Decisions

**Process**:
1. Proposal drafted and shared with community
2. Feedback collected (2-4 weeks)
3. Philosophy audit (does it honor invariants?)
4. Advisory/board review (if established)
5. Final decision by leadership
6. Implementation plan published
7. Review after 6 months

**Examples of Strategic Decisions**:
- Adding a new domain (Domain VII)
- Changing technology platform
- International expansion to new country
- Major partnership that affects identity
- Significant funding acceptance (>$50K)

### For Tactical Decisions

**Process**:
1. Decision made by relevant role/team
2. Documented in changelog
3. Community informed (monthly updates)
4. Tested and evaluated
5. Adjusted based on data

**Examples of Tactical Decisions**:
- UI/UX improvements
- Content topics for the month
- Event scheduling
- Tool/service selection (<$500)
- Hiring a contractor

### For Emergency Decisions

**Process**:
1. Immediate response by leadership
2. Community informed within 24 hours
3. Rationale explained transparently
4. Alternative solutions discussed
5. Retrospective after resolution

**Examples of Emergency Decisions**:
